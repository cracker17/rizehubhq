# RizeHub brand voice

How RizeHub sounds in emails, proposals, reports, social posts, website copy and anything else a client or prospect reads. Client work uses the client's own `brand.md`; this file is for RizeHub's own voice and is the fallback when a client has none.

Our readers: small and mid-size business owners in the US, Australia and the UK. Busy, practical, often burned by agencies before. They want to know what we'll do, what it costs, when it's done, and whether it worked.

## The voice in five words
**Confident. Clear. Warm. Practical. Human.**

- **Confident:** we state what we'll do and stand behind it. No hedging stacks ("we'll try to potentially help").
- **Clear:** plain English, short sentences, one idea per sentence. A 14-year-old could follow it.
- **Warm:** friendly, respectful, on their side. We say "you" more than "we".
- **Practical:** specifics over adjectives. Numbers, steps, dates, links.
- **Human:** sounds like a smart person typing, not a template. Contractions are fine. Vary sentence length.

## Rules
1. Lead with the point. First sentence answers "what is this and why should I care?"
2. Short sentences. Average under 16 words. Paragraphs of 1–3 sentences.
3. Specific beats impressive. "Your product pages load in 1.8 s now, down from 4.2 s" beats "blazing-fast performance".
4. No hype. Never promise rankings, sales, "10x", or guaranteed results.
5. Only real proof. Real numbers, real client results with permission, real reviews. Nothing invented.
6. One ask per message. End with a clear next step.
7. Go easy on em dashes. Use a full stop, comma or colon instead. Max one per short message.
8. Match the client's English: US (color, optimize), AU/UK (colour, optimise). Dates in the client's format.
9. Sign as **RizeHub** (or "The RizeHub team"). No personal names or emails unless the brief says so.
10. Friendly, not casual. No slang, no Taglish, no emojis in emails or proposals. On RizeHub social, max 1–2 emojis per post where they add meaning.

## Words we avoid
unlock, elevate, leverage (as a verb), synergy, game-changer, cutting-edge, revolutionize, world-class, best-in-class, seamless, robust, delve, tapestry, "in today's fast-paced digital landscape", "we are thrilled to announce", "I hope this email finds you well", "don't hesitate to reach out", "take your business to the next level".

## Do / Don't

**Opening an email**
- Don't: "I hope this email finds you well! We are thrilled to share some exciting updates regarding your website."
- Do: "Your new collection pages are live on the preview theme. Here's the link and three things to check."

**Describing a service**
- Don't: "We leverage cutting-edge strategies to elevate your brand and unlock unprecedented growth."
- Do: "We fix slow Shopify stores, write product pages that answer buyer questions, and report what changed every month."

**Reporting a drop**
- Don't: "Traffic experienced some fluctuations this period due to various factors."
- Do: "Organic clicks fell 12% in September. Most of the drop is one blog post that lost its featured snippet. We're rewriting its intro this week to win it back."

**Following up**
- Don't: "Just circling back to touch base on the below. Don't hesitate to reach out!"
- Do: "Quick follow-up on the homepage quote from Tuesday. Want me to hold the October 14 start date for you?"

**Making a claim**
- Don't: "We'll get you to #1 on Google in 30 days, guaranteed."
- Do: "We can't promise rankings. We can promise the fixes Google rewards: faster pages, clearer content, and a monthly report on what moved."

**Social post (RizeHub's own)**
- Don't: "🚀🔥 Ready to take your business to the next level?! Our game-changing web solutions are here! 💯"
- Do: "Most slow Shopify stores have the same 3 problems. Oversized images, too many apps, and a theme nobody's updated since 2022. We check all three in a free 15-minute audit."

**Saying no or delivering bad news**
- Don't: "Unfortunately, we are unable to accommodate this request at this time."
- Do: "That change is outside this month's scope. I can quote it separately, or we can swap it for the blog post planned for week 3. Which works better?"

## Quick self-check before anything goes to the CEO
- Can I cut the first sentence without losing anything? Then cut it.
- Is every number, result and date real and sourced?
- Did I use any word from the avoid list?
- Is there exactly one clear next step?
- Would a busy owner in Ohio, Brisbane or Leeds understand it in one read?
